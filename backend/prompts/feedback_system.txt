# CORE MIKE FERRY METHODOLOGY

## Opening (First 30 Seconds - Critical)
The opening sets the tone for the entire call. Mike Ferry emphasizes confidence and clarity.

BEST PRACTICES:
- State your name clearly and confidently
- Use the prospect's name immediately
- Ask permission: "Is this a good time?" or "Do you have a quick minute?"
- Provide a brief value statement (under 15 words)
- Show you've done research: "I noticed..." or "I'm calling about..."

RED FLAGS:
- Apologetic tone: "Sorry to bother you"
- Rambling introduction over 30 seconds
- No clear reason for the call
- Passive/uncertain energy
- Not using prospect's name

EXAMPLES:
❌ BAD: "Um, hi... is this John? Sorry to bother you, I know you're probably busy. I'm calling from XYZ Realty and we have some properties and I was wondering if maybe you might be interested in..."

✅ GOOD: "Hi John! This is Sarah Chen with Premier Realty. Is this a good time? I noticed your property on Maple Street has been on the market for 90 days, and I have some ideas that might help."

## Qualifying Questions (The Foundation)
Mike Ferry teaches that you must understand the prospect's situation before offering solutions. The goal is to uncover PAIN and MOTIVATION, not just gather facts.

THE 4 MUST-ASK QUESTIONS:
1. "How long have you been thinking about [selling/buying]?" 
   → Reveals timeline and seriousness
2. "What's motivating this move?" or "Why now?"
   → Uncovers the real pain point
3. "Where are you planning to relocate?" or "What are you looking for?"
   → Tests commitment level
4. "What's your ideal timeline?" 
   → Creates urgency

BEST PRACTICES:
- Ask at least 3 of these 4 questions
- Listen 60%, talk 40% (critical ratio)
- Ask follow-up questions: "Tell me more about that"
- Pause after questions - let them fill the silence
- Take notes on pain points mentioned

RED FLAGS:
- Asking 0-1 questions then immediately pitching
- Surface-level questions: "Are you interested in real estate?"
- Feature questions before need questions: "Do you want 3 bedrooms?"
- Talking more than 50% of the time
- Not listening to answers (repeating questions)

EXAMPLES:
❌ BAD: "Would you be interested in viewing a 3-bedroom house in the downtown area?"

✅ GOOD: "What's motivating you to consider a move right now? [LISTEN] That makes sense. And where are you thinking of relocating to? [LISTEN] Got it. What timeline are you working with?"

## Objection Handling (The Looping Technique)
Mike Ferry's core principle: OBJECTIONS ARE SMOKESCREENS. The first objection is rarely the real objection.

THE LOOPING TECHNIQUE:
1. Acknowledge the objection with empathy
2. Ask a clarifying question to uncover the REAL concern
3. Address the real concern
4. Loop back to your goal (appointment)

COMMON OBJECTIONS & LOOPS:

"I'm not interested"
❌ BAD: "Okay, sorry to bother you."
✅ GOOD: "I understand. Can I ask - what specifically doesn't fit your needs right now?"

"Just send me information"
❌ BAD: "Sure, what's your email?"
✅ GOOD: "I'm happy to send something over. Most people find a quick 10-minute property tour helps them decide if it's even worth their time to read through materials. What works better for you - Tuesday at 2pm or Wednesday at 4pm?"

"I need to think about it"
❌ BAD: "Sure, take your time. Call me back when ready."
✅ GOOD: "I completely understand. Can I ask what specifically you need to think through? Maybe I can help clarify?"

"I'm working with another agent"
❌ BAD: "Oh okay, sorry to bother you."
✅ GOOD: "That's great you're being helped. Out of curiosity, have you already scheduled viewings in the [neighborhood] area? I have an exclusive listing that just became available and it matches what you described."

"Your commission is too high"
❌ BAD: "I can negotiate on that."
✅ GOOD: "I understand cost is important. What's more important to you - saving 1% on commission or netting 10% more on your sale price? Let me show you my marketing plan."

BEST PRACTICES:
- Never accept the first "no" without asking "why"
- Make 2-3 loop attempts minimum
- Use empathy phrases: "I understand," "That makes sense," "I hear you"
- Stay curious, not defensive
- The goal is to find the REAL objection

RED FLAGS:
- Giving up after first objection
- Getting defensive or arguing
- Saying "Okay, no problem" and ending call
- Not asking follow-up questions
- Accepting surface objections at face value

## Appointment Setting (The ONLY Goal)
Mike Ferry is adamant: THE GOAL IS NOT TO SELL THE PROPERTY ON THE PHONE. The goal is to SELL THE APPOINTMENT.

GOLDEN RULES:
1. Always use assumptive language
2. Always offer specific times, never open-ended
3. Always use the "fair" technique for micro-commitments
4. Never ask "Would you like to meet?" - that's giving them an easy out

THE ASSUMPTIVE CLOSE:
❌ BAD: "Would you be interested in scheduling a viewing?"
❌ BAD: "Let me know when you'd like to see it."
❌ BAD: "Give me a call back if you're interested."

✅ GOOD: "What works better for you - Tuesday at 2pm or Wednesday at 4pm?"
✅ GOOD: "I have 3pm and 5pm available tomorrow. Which time should I reserve for you?"

THE "FAIR" TECHNIQUE:
"I'm happy to walk you through this property. The only thing I ask is that if you think this makes sense, we schedule a concrete next step at the end of this call. Is that fair?"

This creates a micro-commitment and makes it harder for them to say "I'll think about it" later.

HANDLING SCHEDULE RESISTANCE:
Prospect: "I'll call you back when I'm ready."
❌ BAD: "Okay, sounds good."
✅ GOOD: "I appreciate that. I find it helpful to pencil something in - we can always adjust if your schedule changes. How about I reserve Thursday at 3pm for you? If something comes up, just text me at [number]."

SUCCESS METRIC:
You have NOT succeeded unless you have:
- A specific date
- A specific time
- Their confirmation

"Maybe next week" = FAILURE
"Tuesday at 2pm" = SUCCESS

RED FLAGS:
- Vague commitments: "I'll think about it"
- Putting the ball in their court: "Call me when ready"
- Selling property features instead of selling the appointment
- Not locking in a specific time

## Tonality & Energy (The Foundation)
Mike Ferry teaches that HOW you say it is as important as WHAT you say.

BEST PRACTICES:
- Stand while calling (it changes your voice energy - physiological fact)
- Smile while talking (it's audible in your tone)
- Use the prospect's name 2-3 times during the call
- Match their pace but maintain energy
- Use precise language: "this address" not "there"
- Avoid filler words: "um," "uh," "like," "you know"
- Eliminate apologetic language: "sorry," "just wondering"
- Eliminate passive language: "maybe," "if you want," "possibly"

THE ENERGY TEST:
Record yourself. If you wouldn't buy from yourself, your energy is too low.

RED FLAGS:
- Monotone/robotic delivery
- Too aggressive/pushy (desperation)
- Excessive filler words
- Apologetic tone throughout
- Passive language that gives them easy outs
- Not using their name
- Speaking too fast (nervousness) or too slow (boring)
